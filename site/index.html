RoboSimStudio

Real to sim

RoboSimStudio rebuilds a manipulation task as a physics simulation that people and coding agents can inspect and tune. On the left, the real puzzle is solved by hand. On the right, two YAM arms solve the rebuilt workcell.

Workcell
RSS bench cell with two YAM 6-DoF arms and parallel grippers
Physics
MuJoCo-Warp on GPU at 500 Hz; joint-position control at 50 Hz
Pieces
12 rigid bodies, 55 beads at 25 mm pitch, 12 g per bead, friction 0.65
Case
A free 0.28 kg body; a careless push slides it
Goal
All 55 beads within 4 mm of their sites and still, case level, hands open and clear, held for 2 s
Policy inputs
Four 1280×1024 RGB cameras, joint encoders, CAD of the pieces and case. No object poses, depth or masks.

Piece shapes and the pyramid solution come from the product. The loose layout is set in sim, with Lime starting 70 mm further out than the first layout, which kept it clear of Cyan's hand-to-hand transfer.

Graph-as-Policy

The graph is the policy

Each piece gets a subgraph of typed skill nodes. A node calls robot tools, reads fresh camera frames, and leaves through a named exit such as planned, verified or placed; any refusal routes to abort. After every node, an independent checkpoint reads the simulator to confirm the physical result. The policy never sees those checks. Play the run and the graph follows it.

    Self-refinement

    Every failure pointed to one change

    Skills were rehearsed alone, then in chains of two to six pieces, then all twelve. When a checkpoint failed, the saved RGB frames, robot state and node logs located the condition that broke, the responsible skill changed, and the chain ran again from a fresh start.

    1. Run a chain in simulation
    2. Check each node independently
    3. Locate the failed condition in logs and frames
    4. Change one skill, then rerun

    v13 → this policy

    A finished pyramid was refused

    Failure
    All twelve pieces were seated, but the last node refused and the workflow aborted. Four earlier placement receipts had been flagged as contradicted, and nothing could clear the flag.
    Cause
    The case slid about 1.1 mm while Maroon and Navy went in, and the RGB fit of the case wanders by up to 1.7 mm. Together they crossed the 1.5 mm receipt check once, and the flag was permanent.
    Change
    A flag raised by RGB is re-checked on every later frame with the same thresholds. At the end, a receipt that later frames dispute by less than the 4 mm goal envelope stays valid, and the dispute is recorded.
    Result
    Rerun pending.

    v10 → v13

    A finger hid Cyan's middle bead

    Failure
    Cyan was released, but with the jaw opened to 55 mm a finger still covered its middle bead in the top camera. The check after release couldn't accept Cyan, so the retreat was refused.
    Cause
    Rays from the cameras to 13 points on that bead were traced against the finger mesh at the recorded joint angles. At 65 mm, 11 of 13 were clear; at 75, 80 and 90 mm, all 13.
    Change
    Open to 75 mm, the smallest width that cleared every ray and still passed the collision and joint-limit checks.
    Result
    Cyan was accepted in both frames after opening. Its release checkpoint passed at 0.78 mm.

    layout B → C

    Lime crowded Cyan's transfer

    Failure
    In the first all-twelve run, Cyan's hand-to-hand transfer passed, then the carry stopped. Lime, still loose beside the transfer station, was hidden behind the arms, and its last observation expired.
    Cause
    The carry planner refuses to sweep a hand through space whose occupancy it can't currently see. With Lime out of view, that space was unknown.
    Change
    Start Lime 70 mm further from the case. This changes the declared initial layout, not the policy.
    Result
    Cyan's carry and release passed, and two pieces later Lime was found, grasped and placed from its new start.
